Run the whole route from one app

OpenPoolPro is free, open-source software for pool service companies. Your technicians work their route on a phone. You run customers, chemistry, invoices and QuickBooks billing from the office. It all lives on a server you control, in one Docker container.

Up and running this afternoon

Run one command on your server.

Any VPS with Docker, x86 or ARM. Use the bundled Caddy for HTTPS, or put it behind Pangolin, Traefik or Nginx. The app, the database and the nightly backups live in one folder.

Then answer the setup questions.

Your name and password, two-step sign-in, your business details, your prices and your colours. There is no default login to change later.

Hand your crew their phones.

Each technician gets a temporary password and their own route. Add the app to the home screen and it works like a native one, signal or not.

$ mkdir openpoolpro && cd openpoolpro
$ curl -O https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dlommm/OpenPoolPro/main/docker-compose.caddy.yml
$ printf 'SECRET_KEY=%s\nDOMAIN=pools.example.com\n' "$(openssl rand -hex 32)" > .env
$ docker compose -f docker-compose.caddy.yml up -d
$ docker compose -f docker-compose.caddy.yml logs app | grep "Setup code"
No owner account yet. Setup code: 9SZS-GBEK-82NZ

Everything a pool company runs on

A visit in six steps

Arrival, water, chemicals, tasks, photos and notes, then finish. Readings show low, in range or high as the tech types, in words, not just colour.

Dose suggestions come from your targets. Nothing counts as added until the tech records it.

Every customer on one page

Properties, pools and spas, gate codes and dogs, equipment and warranties, service history, water trends, invoices and contracts.

Customers get their own portal link for visit reports, photos and invoices.

Billing that QuickBooks collects

Finished visits and jobs become monthly drafts. You review and approve them; QuickBooks Online invoices the customer and takes the payment.

Prefer your own invoices? Branded PDFs, sales tax, receivables by age and payment reminders are built in.

Routes you can see

Each technician's day on a map, in stop order. Move a stop to another route, or let the balancer even out the week.

Owners can keep a route of their own, set by hand.

Numbers worth reading

Revenue by month and where it came from, revenue per visit, customers gained and lost, and the properties whose chemical costs have outgrown their rate.

Every chart has a table view and exports to CSV, Excel or PDF.

Your colours, light or dark

Pick a palette or your own brand colour; every shade is checked so text stays readable. Customers see it on the portal and in emails.

Each person chooses light or dark, text size and density for themselves.

Your server, your data

No subscription and no per-technician fee. Customer records never leave a machine you own, and nothing phones home.

  • No default password. The first owner is created in setup, proven by a code from the server log.
  • Two-step sign-in with backup codes, offered on day one and required by policy if you like.
  • Nightly backups, plus an optional copy to a NAS or cloud folder.
  • Everything logged: sign-ins, money changes and settings, in an audit log only owners can read.
  • Updates are one pull. Migrations run on start, after a backup.

Common questions

Nothing. OpenPoolPro is free and open source under the AGPL-3.0 licence. You pay only for the server you run it on; a small VPS is plenty for a company with a handful of technicians.

A Linux machine with Docker, x86 or ARM, from any provider, and a domain name. There is a compose file for a bare VPS (it brings its own HTTPS), one for Pangolin, one for any other proxy and one for Unraid. The deploy guide walks through each.

It's an installable web app. Open it on the phone, choose Add to Home Screen, and it opens full screen like a native app. Visits finished without signal are saved on the phone and sent when it's back.

Yes, QuickBooks Online. You approve each month's charges in OpenPoolPro and QuickBooks invoices the customer and collects payment. A CSV export is there too if you'd rather import by hand.

In a single folder on your server: one SQLite database plus photos, PDFs and nightly backups. Copy the folder and you have everything. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you connect email or QuickBooks.

Each release is published to Docker Hub and the GitHub container registry. Run docker compose pull, then up -d. The app backs up the database before it changes anything.

Yes. Technicians see their own route and customers and none of the money. You can grant individual permissions, such as seeing all routes, managing customers, marking cash or approving billing.
